Piedigrotta Church is a cave chapel carved into soft sedimentary rock directly above the sea – one of the most remarkable sacred sites in Calabria. It was created out of gratitude by shipwrecked sailors and then expanded over decades by local sculptors who carved dozens of biblical figures into the rock. It brings together nature, faith and folk art and is inseparable from the seafaring identity of Pizzo.

The shipwreck legend – how the church was born

According to local tradition, at the end of the 17th century a sailing ship with a crew from Torre del Greco near Naples was wrecked off the coast of Pizzo; the legend is also linked to the ship’s bell dated 1632 – older than the wreck itself. As waves smashed the hull, the captain and crew prayed before a painting of Our Lady of Piedigrotta hanging in the captain’s cabin – a Neapolitan devotion that gave the chapel its name – vowing to build a chapel in her honour if they survived. The ship sank, but every sailor reached the shore alive.

On the beach, among the wreckage, they found the surviving painting of the Madonna and the ship’s bell. They took it as a divine sign – placed the painting in a small grotto hollowed into the cliff, and so the Chiesetta di Piedigrotta was born. The image of the Madonna di Piedigrotta remains the centre of devotion; however, to be precise, after restoration the original painting was not always displayed inside the grotto itself, and the altar now holds a copy.

The local legend needs to be separated from the later, better-documented history of the site. The tradition of the shipwreck and the painting of the Virgin relates to the origins of the sanctuary, while the current appearance of the interior is the result of sculptural work carried out in the 19th and 20th centuries. A document of 1725 names Giovanni Benedetto of Pizzo and his son Antonio as founders (1675). Angelo Barone began the main work around 1880 and continued it until his death in 1915; after him it was continued by his son Alfonso Barone, and in 1969 Angelo’s grandson Giorgio Barone restored some of the sculptures and added new elements.

Sculptures from rock – the work of the Barone family

For nearly two centuries, the chapel remained a modest place of prayer. The main expansion of the interior began only at the end of the 19th century, when Angelo Barone started enlarging the grotto and carving figures in it – while digging, he deliberately left blocks of rock that he later turned into statues. Working by hand in the soft sedimentary rock, he carved saints, scenes from Scripture, and episodes from the life of Christ.

After Angelo, the work was continued by his son Alfonso Barone, and in 1969 Alfonso’s son Giorgio Barone, back from Canada, restored some of the damaged sculptures and carved two medallions: Pope John XXIII and John F. Kennedy. Thanks to successive generations, the grotto was further deepened, shaped and gradually filled with figures. What you see today is the result of many stages of work from the late 19th to the second half of the 20th century.

Inside, dozens of figures carved directly from the rock depict scenes from the life of Jesus, saints and figures of local devotion. Some sculptures are fully three-dimensional, others take the form of reliefs, and the grotto itself retains an irregular character with successive chambers and niches hollowed out in stages. Alongside the figures, frescoes attributed to Alfonso Barone have also survived, including scenes described as a pilgrimage to Lourdes, a ship in a storm, the Battle of Lepanto, the marriage of the Madonna, and a round fresco of the Ascension. Many of these paintings are now in poor condition because the interior has long been exposed to damp and salt carried in from the sea.

Among the most notable scenes:

  • The Nativity – a crib scene with Mary, Joseph and shepherds carved into a natural rock niche
  • St Francis of Paola – patron saint of Calabria, shown during his miraculous crossing of the Strait of Messina on his own cloak
  • The multiplication of the loaves and fishes – one of the biblical scenes carved in the grotto
  • Madonna di Piedigrotta – the image surrounded by local veneration, set in the central altar niche

Descriptions of the interior also mention Our Lady of Pompeii, St Anthony of Padua with orphans, the Sacred Heart of Jesus and the Immaculate Heart of Mary, Our Lady of Lourdes, St George slaying the dragon, and St Rita. This selection captures the character of Piedigrotta well: it is not decoration following a single programme, but a collection of sculptures that grew over many years, where Gospel scenes stand alongside locally venerated saints and Marian figures.

The rock from which the cliff is made is a soft sedimentary rock – popularly called tuff (tufo), and in newer descriptions sandstone or calcarenite (arenaria, calcarenite) – which made it possible to hollow and carve the interior by hand. It is precisely this property that allowed successive creators to gradually hollow out chambers and give the figures their characteristic softness of form.

Visiting and practical information

Piedigrotta Church lies about 1.5–2 km north of the historic centre of Pizzo, along the coastal road SS522 / Via Riviera Prangi. Access is via steps down to the beach, so comfortable shoes really are useful. From Piazza della Repubblica, the drive takes about 5 minutes, and the walk usually 20–30 minutes. The way down is easy, but the walk back up to the clifftop centre is steep – in summer, save it for late afternoon.

Allow 15–30 minutes for the interior itself. Even at the height of summer it stays pleasantly cool inside; photography is allowed without flash. A standard ticket costs €5, reduced €3.50, and a combined ticket with Castello Murat (the Aragonese castle) €8 / €6.50 reduced (as of June 2026). From 1 March to 31 October the church is open 9:00–13:00 and 15:00–18:00; in winter it closes at 17:00. Prices and hours change seasonally – check them before your visit.

The Festa della Madonna di Piedigrotta takes place on 2 July and is preceded by a novena. The most distinctive moment is the sea procession: the image of the Madonna travels by boat on the “Perla Nera”, decorated with flowers and small flags. This is when it becomes clearest that this is not merely a tourist attraction but a living sanctuary rooted in the maritime religiosity of Pizzo.

Chiesa di Piedigrotta is one of the most recognisable places of worship and symbols of Pizzo, combining popular religiosity, memory of the sea and the votive traditions of seafarers. For locals it is not just an extraordinary grotto above the beach but also part of the town's maritime identity.

After your visit, head to Castello Murat in the town centre and end your walk on Piazza della Repubblica with a tartufo di Pizzo.

Piedigrotta Church – frequently asked questions

Who carved the statues in Piedigrotta Church?

The main work was begun in the second half of the 19th century by Angelo Barone. After him, the work was developed by his son Alfonso Barone, and in the late 1960s Giorgio Barone restored some of the damaged sculptures and carved two medallions: Pope John XXIII and John F. Kennedy. The figures were carved by hand in the soft sedimentary rock.

How was the cave church created?

According to local tradition, the origins of the sanctuary are linked to a shipwreck at the end of the 17th century; the legend is also connected with the ship’s bell dated 1632. The surviving sailors from Torre del Greco are said to have placed a painting of the Virgin Mary from their ship in a grotto. The simple chapel was later expanded and sculpted in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Is Piedigrotta Church open all year?

Yes, but hours are seasonal: from 1 March to 31 October 9:00–13:00 and 15:00–18:00, in winter until 17:00. Admission is paid – €5 (reduced €3.50), combined ticket with Castello Murat €8 (reduced €6.50), as of June 2026.

When does the sea procession of the Madonna di Piedigrotta take place?

The Festa della Madonna di Piedigrotta takes place on 2 July and is preceded by a novena. In the sea procession, the image travels by boat on the “Perla Nera”, decorated with flowers and small flags.
