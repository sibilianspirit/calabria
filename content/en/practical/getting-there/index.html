Calabria sits at the very tip of the Italian boot, but getting there is easier than you might think. The fastest option is flying to Lamezia Terme – flights from Europe usually take 2–3 hours. Alternatively, take a train from Rome (around 4 hours) or drive south – the motorway from Salerno is toll-free.

Flying to Calabria

Calabria has three airports, but in practice only one really matters – Lamezia Terme (SUF). It is the region's main airport, centrally located on the western coast with good access to Tropea, Cosenza and Reggio Calabria.

The route network at Lamezia Terme changes seasonally, but this is where you will find the most domestic and international flights in the region. In practice, the most important are domestic connections to major Italian airports and seasonal as well as year-round services from selected European cities.

Direct flights from Poland

Poland is one of Calabria's best-connected foreign markets, although almost every route is seasonal (late March to late October). In summer 2026 flights take 2–2.5 hours:

Katowice–Reggio Calabria is the only Polish route to Reggio. Ryanair dropped it for winter 2026/27 when its incentive agreement with the airport expired, even though flights were 89% full in 2025; the last flight of the season is on 22 October 2026. Ryanair operating days are taken from the airline’s September 2026 timetable and Wizz Air days from airport announcements; the summer 2027 timetable is not yet published, so always check your date on the airline’s website. In winter, other options are one-stop itineraries via Milan Bergamo or Malpensa (Ryanair flies to both Lamezia and Reggio daily all year), via Rome Fiumicino (ITA Airways to Lamezia), or a flight to Catania followed by FlixBus to Villa San Giovanni (about 2 h 40 min, including the ferry).

  • Ryanair – the largest carrier at Lamezia and Reggio, with flights from Poland and from Milan, Bologna, Turin and other northern Italian cities.
  • Wizz Air – summer flights from Katowice and Warsaw to Lamezia.
  • easyJet – offers selected connections, primarily on high-demand routes.
  • ITA Airways – domestic flights, convenient especially for intercontinental connections.
  • Eurowings, Lufthansa, Condor – selected direct connections from Germany, depending on the season.

In practice, the best airport choice depends on your travel plan. Lamezia Terme (SUF) is most convenient for the Tyrrhenian coast and the central part of the region: Tropea is about 55–65 km and usually 1–1.25 h by car, Pizzo about 30 km and 25–35 min, Cosenza about 65–70 km and around 1 h. From Lamezia to Reggio Calabria takes usually 1 h 45 min–2 h.

Reggio Calabria Tito Minniti Airport (REG) is much smaller and works best for trips to the far south of the region or onward to Sicily. Reggio city centre is only 4–5 km away, about 10–15 min drive; Scilla is about 25 km and 30–40 min; Villa San Giovanni about 15 km and 20–25 min.

From Reggio Calabria airport

  • ATAM city bus – connects the airport with the centre and Reggio Calabria Centrale station roughly every 30 minutes. The ride takes about 15 minutes and a ticket costs €1.50, valid for 75 minutes. A separate ATAM service runs a few times a day to the port, where hydrofoils leave for Messina.
  • Train – Reggio Calabria Aeroporto station is about 1 km from the terminal, linked by a free shuttle. Regional trains run to Reggio Centrale, Villa San Giovanni and Scilla.
  • Taxi – the city sets fixed fares from the airport: roughly €20 to Centrale station and €30 to the port. Confirm the price with the driver before setting off.
  • Car – rental desks are in the arrivals hall and at the airport exit. Scilla is about half an hour away on the A2.

From Lamezia Terme airport

  • Lamezia Airlink – the bus to Lamezia Terme Centrale station runs roughly every 30 minutes from early morning to late evening. A ticket costs €1.80 and the stop is about 50 m from the arrivals exit. Trenitalia sells combined bus and train tickets.
  • Train from Centrale – regional trains run to Pizzo, Tropea, Paola and Reggio Calabria; Tropea is about an hour away.
  • Long-distance buses – Autolinee Romano runs from the airport to Cosenza, Catanzaro and Crotone; Cosenza takes 45–60 minutes. In summer a few private shuttles also serve Tropea and Capo Vaticano on a pre-booking basis.
  • Taxi – there is no municipal tariff, but local companies quote fixed prices: about €50 to Pizzo and €80 to Tropea. Book ahead, especially for late-evening arrivals.

Crotone Sant'Anna Airport (CRV) has a limited, seasonally variable route network. It is practical for the central-eastern Ionian coast: Crotone is about 15 km and around 20 min, Le Castella similar, and Catanzaro Lido about 50 km and around 1 h.

From any of the airports, the most convenient way to explore the region is by rental car. Note that Lamezia Terme Centrale station is not at the terminal but 2–3 km away – over half an hour on foot with luggage, so take the Airlink bus instead.

By train

The train is a good option if you are already in Italy – especially for routes along the Tyrrhenian coast. Calabria is served by Trenitalia high-speed trains and private operator Italo.

Main railway stations in Calabria:

  • Lamezia Terme Centrale – the main interchange, served by both high-speed and regional trains.
  • Cosenza – the station for northern Calabria.
  • Reggio Calabria Centrale – the terminus of the Rome–south line, right in the centre next to the Lungomare promenade.
  • Paola – a small station west of Cosenza where some high-speed trains stop.

From Cosenza station, you can continue on Ferrovie della Calabria trains to Cosenza Centro. These services also reach Rogliano and Marzi, making it easier to travel within the city and farther south of Cosenza.

This is important when planning: not all popular destinations are on the main line. Tropea has its own station on the Tyrrhenian line, but high-speed trains do not go there – you usually need to change to a regional train at Lamezia Terme Centrale or Rosarno. Scilla has its own railway station, while for Pizzo the nearest station is Vibo Valentia-Pizzo, about 10 km from the historic centre; for places on the Ionian coast such as Gerace or Stilo, you will usually also need a bus or car.

The cheapest promotional allocations are limited and may disappear quickly, so check the price and availability for your specific date in the operator's booking system.

The train journey from Rome to Lamezia Terme on a Trenitalia or Italo high-speed service follows the Tyrrhenian coast after Salerno. When travelling south, views of the sea and mountains are usually better from the left side of the carriage.

Regional services vary greatly: there are more trains on the main sections, while services to some inland towns and parts of the Ionian coast are less frequent. Always check the timetable for your particular route. For mountain towns and less well-connected areas, a rental car is more convenient.

By car

Driving makes sense if you plan a longer stay and want complete freedom. From Rome, follow the motorway corridor south via the Naples and Salerno area, then continue on the A2 towards Calabria. In practice, this is the simplest route for most drivers travelling from central Italy.

Approximate distances from Rome:

  • Rome → Cosenza – 520 km, about 5 h
  • Rome → Lamezia Terme – 580 km, about 5 h 30 min
  • Rome → Tropea – 620 km, about 7–8 h
  • Rome → Reggio Calabria – 700 km, about 6 h 30 min

Tolls (pedaggio). The A1 (Rome–Naples) and the stretch to Salerno are tolled – expect to pay around €20–25 for a car on the Rome–Salerno stretch. The good news: the A2 motorway from Salerno to Reggio Calabria is completely toll-free – that is over 400 km of free, well-maintained dual carriageway.

For a car using 6.5 l/100 km, the drive from Rome to Lamezia (about 580 km) means consuming roughly 38 litres of fuel. At a petrol price of around €2.10/l (the Calabrian average in September 2026), that works out to roughly €80, and after adding motorway tolls the total cost comes to approximately €100–105 one way. For Reggio Calabria the fuel cost will be higher, as the distance rises to about 700 km.

Tips for drivers:

  • Fuel on the A2 – there are numerous filling stations and service areas along the route, but fuel prices can vary considerably between the motorway, towns and individual stations. Compare current prices before filling up.
  • Telepass is not necessary – on the tolled sections, a credit card or cash will do.
  • Speed cameras are frequent – both on the A2 (130 km/h limit, 80–110 km/h in roadwork zones) and on the SS106 coastal road.
  • Local roads – mountain roads to villages in Aspromonte, Sila and Pollino are winding and narrow. You do not need an SUV, but a small car with good brakes makes life much easier.

If you would rather not drive your own car from home, consider flying to Lamezia Terme and renting a car on arrival – it is usually cheaper and faster than a 1,500+ km drive across Europe.

By bus

The bus is the cheapest option, but also the slowest. FlixBus runs regular services from northern Italy and abroad to Calabria's main cities.

On the Milan–Calabria and Turin–Calabria routes, many services are overnight, typically taking 10–14 hours depending on the destination. Long-distance carriers usually include 1 carry-on and 1 checked bag in the fare, but excess luggage may be charged, so check the limits when booking. In the summer season and on holiday weekends, southbound buses can sell out several days ahead.

Key FlixBus routes to Calabria:

  • Rome → Cosenza – about 5–6 h, from €15
  • Rome → Lamezia Terme – about 6–7 h, from €15
  • Milan → Cosenza – about 10–12 h (overnight service), from €25
  • Naples → Cosenza – about 3–4 h, from €10

Cosenza is an important bus hub in the region, and Lamezia Terme Aeroporto also has a FlixBus stop, making transfers easier. Buses run between Cosenza and Lamezia, but the journey usually takes about 1–1.5 hours, so check the specific timetable before travelling.

Regional buses (Romano, Ferrovie della Calabria) serve routes within Calabria, but schedules are unreliable – especially at weekends and outside the summer season. Do not count on buses as your main transport around the region.

Practical tips

Which transport to choose? It depends on where you are coming from and what you plan to do once there.

The train works best along the Tyrrhenian axis: Paola, Lamezia Terme, Vibo Valentia-Pizzo, Tropea, Rosarno, Villa San Giovanni and Reggio Calabria. A car is much better if you want to see Sila, Aspromonte, Pollino or towns like Morano Calabro, Gerace, Bova or Stilo. On the Ionian coast, rail and bus services are generally less frequent than on the Tyrrhenian side.

General advice:

  • Book train and bus tickets in advance online. Prices rise closer to the date, and the cheapest promotional fares sell out first.
  • In high season (July–August) book early. This applies to flights but also to car hire – prices can increase 2–3 times.
  • Lamezia Terme airport is centrally located. It is a good starting point for many places on the Tyrrhenian coast and in the central part of the region.
  • Ferry from Sicily. The main port for cars is Villa San Giovanni; the ferry crossing to Messina takes about 20 minutes. Pedestrians can also use connections from Reggio Calabria, for which you should check the current timetable and sailing time. Villa San Giovanni is about 15 km from Reggio Calabria and is a key road and rail hub for traffic to Sicily.
  • You will need a car on the ground. Public transport within Calabria is limited. Beaches, mountain villages and smaller attractions are only comfortably reachable by car. See our car rental guide for details.

Getting to Calabria – frequently asked questions

Which airport is closest to Tropea?

Lamezia Terme airport (SUF) – about 55–65 km from Tropea, usually 1–1.25 h by car. You can also get there via Lamezia Terme Centrale station and then by regional train to Tropea.

How much does the train from Rome to Calabria cost?

Train ticket prices from Rome to Calabria depend on the date, the service chosen and the availability of promotional fares. Super Economy is one of Trenitalia's promotional offers, while Italo has its own fares. Check the price online for your specific date at trenitalia.com or italotreno.it.

Is the motorway to Calabria tolled?

Partly. The A1 Rome–Naples stretch and the section to Salerno are tolled (around €20–25). However, the A2 from Salerno to Reggio Calabria – over 400 km – is completely toll-free.

How do I get from Lamezia Terme airport to Tropea?

The easiest way is by rental car. Alternatively, take a shuttle or taxi to Lamezia Terme Centrale station, then a regional train to Tropea. The entire journey, including the airport–station transfer and waiting for the train, is best counted as roughly 1.5–2 hours rather than just the rail travel time.

Are there direct flights from Poland to Calabria?

Yes. In summer 2026 Ryanair flies to Lamezia Terme from Kraków, Wrocław, Katowice and Warsaw-Modlin, and Wizz Air from Katowice and Warsaw Chopin. Ryanair also flies Katowice–Reggio Calabria. In winter 2026/27 only Kraków and Wrocław keep direct flights to Lamezia.

Should I fly into Lamezia Terme or Reggio Calabria?

Lamezia Terme for Tropea, Pizzo, Capo Vaticano, Cosenza, the Sila and Catanzaro. Reggio Calabria for Reggio itself, Scilla, the Costa Viola, Aspromonte, Bova and onward travel to Sicily. The two airports are about 130 km apart, roughly 1.5 hours on the A2.
