A rental car is by far the most convenient way to explore Calabria. Public transport exists but is sparse and unreliable beyond the main rail lines. A car gives you complete freedom – from the beaches of Tropea to the mountain villages of Aspromonte.

Where to Rent a Car

The main pickup point is Lamezia Terme Airport (SUF) – Calabria's largest airport with international flights. Rental offices and car parks are about 100 m from the terminal, on the right after leaving the building. According to airport operator SACAL, 11 companies operate here: Autovia, Avis, Drivalia, Europcar, Hertz, Italy Car Rent, Locauto, Maggiore, Noleggiare, Sicily by Car and Sixt. Comparison sites also list off-airport firms (such as Ecovia or Target Rent) – often cheaper, but you need a shuttle to their office, which adds time after a late-night arrival.

The second airport is Reggio Calabria Tito Minniti Airport (REG) – smaller, with flights from Milan, Turin, Bologna and other Italian cities, plus Katowice (Ryanair) until 22 October 2026. SACAL lists only three rental companies here: Noleggiare, Sicily by Car and Sixt, with offices and car parks at the airport exit, about 250 m from the terminal. Other firms (such as Autovia on Via Ravagnese Superiore) hand over cars off-site, a few minutes' walk away. Always check the pickup location on your voucher. For flights and transfers, see our guide to getting to Calabria.

Calabria also has a third passenger airport: Crotone Sant'Anna (CRV), serving mainly domestic and seasonal flights. Only Avis and Violauto operate there, with desks in the arrivals hall and cars parked just outside the terminal. Outside airports, cars can also be picked up at Lamezia Terme Centrale and Reggio Calabria Centrale railway stations and at city-centre offices, though the latter often have shorter opening hours than airport desks.

Key booking tips:

  • Book online in advance – walk-up rates at the airport desk are usually higher, and in August small cars can sell out. See where to compare offers below.
  • Choose a small car – Calabrian town streets are often very narrow and parking spaces tight. A Fiat 500 or Panda is the ideal choice.
  • Payment card for the security deposit – it must be in the main driver's name. Debit cards are hit and miss: Locauto accepts named debit cards, Hertz only for some car groups, and Sicily by Car requires you to buy its protection package. Digital cards on your phone are usually refused. Use the “debit card accepted” filter on comparison sites.
  • Driving licence – a Polish driving licence, issued in the EU, is sufficient. The minimum age depends on the rental company and car category: it is often 21, but some companies rent to drivers from 18. Drivers under 25 usually pay a surcharge or have a more limited choice of cars.
  • Check fuel and mileage policy – the most common arrangement is full-to-full. Most offers include unlimited mileage, but cheaper local rates may mean a limit of 100–150 km/day or surcharges for ferries and leaving the region.
  • Inspect the car before driving off – photograph every scratch and dent. Make sure existing damage is recorded on the collection report.

What to look at besides the price:

  • Broker cover is not the same as desk cover. A package bought from a comparison site works as reimbursement: after damage the rental company charges the excess to your card and the broker pays you back once you send the paperwork. The full deposit is still blocked on your card. Desk staff will push their own package – if you already have cover, you can decline.
  • Office location – “in terminal” versus “shuttle” can mean half an hour’s difference, especially in Reggio.
  • Fuel policy – choose full-to-full. If you return the car short of fuel, Sicily by Car charges a €25 refuelling fee plus airport charges and VAT on top of the fuel.
  • Reviews of the specific branch, not the whole chain – queues and damage disputes depend on the location.
  • Ferry permission if you plan to visit Sicily (see the FAQ).

Costs, Deposits and Insurance

Prices depend heavily on the dates, rental length, airport, car category and level of cover. As a guide: in September 2026, DiscoverCars showed average prices for a small car at Lamezia Terme of roughly €10–15 a day in November and about €35 a day in August. Reggio is usually a little more expensive, as there is less competition. In high season prices climb week by week, so book August rentals in spring – ideally with free cancellation, so you can switch if a cheaper deal appears.

Deposit and excess

A cheap daily rate almost always comes with a high excess (franchigia) – the amount you pay in case of damage or theft. Sicily by Car’s terms are a good example: a €200 deposit for small and mid-size cars, but a damage excess of €1,200–1,500 plus VAT, and €1,600–2,000 plus VAT for theft. Locauto takes no deposit on passenger cars, just a token card check. For premium cars and vans, blocks of €1,000 or more are common – check your card limit before you travel.

There are three ways to protect yourself:

  • The rental company’s package (SCDW, Super Cover, zero excess) – the most expensive, but it usually lowers the deposit and settles things on the spot.
  • The comparison site’s package – cheaper; after damage you pay the rental company and claim the money back from the broker.
  • A standalone car hire excess policy bought at home, e.g. from RentalCover or a travel insurer. Compare its limit with the excess in your contract – some add-ons to travel policies cover only a few thousand euros.

On top of the base rate, expect:

  • Fuel – in mid-September 2026 the average self-service price in Calabria, according to the MIMIT ministry, was about €2.13/l for petrol and €2.24/l for diesel, above the national average. Servito (attended) pumps cost more, often by 10–20 cents a litre. The Osservaprezzi Carburanti service shows the cheapest stations nearby.
  • Additional driver – the charge depends on the company and selected offer; check it before booking.
  • GPS – availability and charges depend on the rental company. You can use offline maps on your phone.
  • Young driver – drivers under 25 often pay a surcharge, and available car categories may be limited.
  • After-hours pickup or return – if the office has shorter hours, the rental company may charge a fee for service outside opening hours.

Speeding and ZTL fines usually arrive months later: the rental company passes the driver’s details to the police or municipality, which then writes to you. For years companies also added their own “administrative fee”. In September 2025 Italy’s competition and consumer authority (AGCM) fined Sicily by Car €8 million and Locauto €400,000 over such automatic charges. If an unexplained fee appears on your card after the trip, ask the company for the contractual basis.

Driving in Calabria – What You Need to Know

Traffic outside the largest cities is usually lighter than in Naples or Rome, but local roads still require careful driving. Drivers move at a brisk pace, and mountain routes demand patience. A few things to keep in mind:

A2 Motorway (Salerno–Reggio Calabria) – the main north-south artery. The road is in good condition after years of renovation. Crucially, it is toll-free along its entire length, from Salerno to Reggio Calabria. Speed limit is 130 km/h, but frequent construction zones reduce it to 80–110 km/h. Speed cameras are widespread.

SS106 Jonica (Ionian coast) – the state road running along the eastern coast from Reggio Calabria to Taranto (491 km). The SS106 has been and continues to be upgraded, but for much of its Calabrian route it remains a single-carriageway road. Dual-carriageway sections occur only on selected stretches. The road can be hazardous in bad weather – localised flooding occurs.

Mountain roads – routes leading to villages in Aspromonte, Sila or Pollino are winding, narrow and steep. You do not need to avoid them, but:

  • Drive slowly and honk before blind bends.
  • In winter (December–March), passes above 1,000 m may have snow and ice – check whether your rental includes snow chains.
  • Fuel stations in the mountains are scarce, and in small towns they are often closed on Sundays and over lunch (roughly 13:00–16:00). Only the self-service machines work then, so carry a physical payment card or cash. Ideally, fill up on the coast.
  • GPS navigation can be unreliable – follow road signs too. Download offline maps before setting off, as signal fades in the mountains.

Useful apps: VAI Anas Plus shows real-time traffic, accidents and closures on ANAS roads, including the A2 and SS106. The Polizia di Stato publishes a list of fixed speed cameras and a weekly schedule of mobile checks by region. In many towns you can pay for parking with EasyPark.

In Italy, dipped headlights must be used at all times outside built-up areas. A warning triangle and reflective vest (for anyone leaving the vehicle on the carriageway or hard shoulder) are mandatory in the car. If you are travelling with children, remember that children under 150 cm tall must use an appropriate child seat or booster according to their weight and height.

Speed limits: 50 km/h in built-up areas, 90 km/h on state roads outside towns, 110 km/h on dual carriageway expressways, 130 km/h on motorways. Fines are steep: from 40 € for minor speeding to several hundred euros for serious violations.

Motorways, Parking and ZTL

Tolls: The A2 motorway (managed by ANAS) is toll-free along its entire length from Salerno to Reggio Calabria, so you pay nothing in Calabria, Basilicata or Campania. Tolls start only north of Salerno (the A3 to Naples, then the A1). At toll booths on paying motorways you can pay by card, cash or contactless. Telepass is not standard in rental cars – its availability depends on the company and pickup point.

Parking:

  • White lines – parking is usually free, but always check vertical signs and supplementary panels: a time limit may apply, with a parking disc (disco orario) displayed behind the windscreen.
  • Blue lines – paid parking (meters or the EasyPark app). Typically 0.50–1.50 €/hour.
  • Yellow lines – reserved for residents, disabled or official vehicles. Do not park here.
  • In smaller resort towns (Tropea, Pizzo, Scilla), finding a spot in the centre during summer can be difficult. Look for car parks on the outskirts and walk down.

Parking in the most popular towns:

  • Scilla – from 5 April to 31 October, parking is paid on Lungomare Colombo (Marina Grande) and Via De Marco above Chianalea: €2.50 an hour or €15 a day, daily 8:00–24:00, via meters or the Vivi Scilla and EasyPark apps. All of Chianalea is a camera-controlled ZTL.
  • Tropea – new traffic and parking rules apply from July 2026, and the historic centre is a camera-controlled ZTL. Do not drive in without a permit: park on the edge of town and, if your accommodation is inside the zone, ask your host about a permit before arrival.
  • Pizzo – the historic centre has a restricted traffic zone. On summer evenings it is easiest to park in the upper part of town and walk down.

ZTL (Zona Traffico Limitato): These are restricted traffic zones marked with a white "zona traffico limitato" sign showing the hours of operation. In practice you need to read the sign very carefully, as the zone may only apply in the evenings, at weekends or seasonally in summer. Cameras read number plates automatically, and if your hotel is inside the ZTL, it may sometimes be able to register your licence plate – but this must be arranged in advance.

In Calabria, you will encounter ZTL mainly in Reggio Calabria (including Corso Garibaldi, active 24 hours), Cosenza (cameras on 11 central streets since July 2026, active 24 hours), Catanzaro, Tropea and Scilla. An unauthorised entry typically results in a fine starting from about 83 €, and the rental company may still try to add its own fee for passing on your details. This is one of the most common causes of unpleasant extra charges after returning from holiday.

Alternatives to Renting a Car

If you prefer not to drive, you have a few options – though none offers the same freedom as your own car:

Trains (Trenitalia Regionale): The Tyrrhenian axis Reggio Calabria–Lamezia Terme works well, and connections to Tropea run on a regional line via Pizzo/Vibo Valentia and Rosarno rather than the main long-distance line. Cosenza is not on the coastal line – it is reached by a branch heading inland. Approximate journey times: Lamezia Terme Centrale–Tropea about 1 hour, Lamezia–Reggio Calabria Centrale about 1 h 30 min–2 h, Reggio Calabria Centrale–Scilla about 20–30 minutes.

The key railway junction of the region is Lamezia Terme Centrale, connected to SUF Airport by the Lamezia AirLink / Autolinee Lamezia Multiservizi bus. Tickets for this shuttle are available from the Edicola/Tabacchi point inside the terminal. The Ionian line runs less frequently, and many inland destinations require a change to a bus. More on connections in our getting there guide.

Regional buses: Ferrovie della Calabria, Romano Autolinee and local operators serve smaller towns, but timetables are thin (sometimes just 1–2 services per day) and unreliable. Weekend and holiday services are often suspended.

Taxis and NCC: Available in larger cities. More expensive than in northern Italy but still reasonable for short trips (e.g. Lamezia Airport–Tropea about 70–90 €). Always agree on the price before getting in.

Outside Lamezia, Reggio Calabria, Cosenza and the larger stations, taxis are scarce. In Tropea, Scilla or Pizzo, outside peak season and outside the summer season, you often need to book a ride by phone or through your hotel in advance. Ride-hailing apps such as Uber are not a common or reliable option here.

Chauffeur service (NCC): A premium option, popular for airport transfers or day trips. Prices from 150 €/day.

If you plan to visit more than one coastline or explore mountain villages, renting a car is the most convenient option. Calabria has two long coastlines – Tyrrhenian and Ionian – separated by mountainous terrain, and many interesting places lie beyond the main rail lines: from mountain villages to beaches between stations. Travelling between the Costa degli Dei and the Ionian coast by public transport can be multi-step and significantly slower than driving. More trip planning tips in our practical guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an International Driving Permit in Calabria?

Not if you hold an EU-issued driving licence. A Polish driving licence is fully accepted. For drivers from outside the EU, rules depend on the country of issue and the rental company's policy: for the US and Canada an IDP is often required or strongly recommended, while for short stays with a UK licence the same rule does not apply. Always check the specific company's conditions before booking.

Is full insurance worth it?

Standard insurance (CDW) includes an excess (franchigia), which may be charged to the driver in the event of damage. A third-party excess policy may reimburse eligible excess costs after a claim, but it does not replace the rental company's terms or guarantee that the deposit will be waived. Check exclusions and the claims procedure before buying.

How does refuelling work in Italy?

Petrol stations have two modes: self-service (you pump and pay at the machine or inside) and servito (an attendant fills the tank – 10–20 cents per litre more expensive). On Sundays and at night, many stations operate self-service only, accepting banknotes and cards at automated pumps. Motorway stations are open 24/7.

Can I take my rental car to Sicily?

It depends on the company. Maggiore explicitly allows ferry crossings to the islands, as does Locauto, although damage during the crossing is not covered by basic protection. Other companies vary by rate, and leaving the region may carry a surcharge. Have the permission written into your contract at pickup. Car ferries to Messina leave from Villa San Giovanni, about 30 minutes’ drive from Reggio airport.

Can I rent a car in Calabria with a debit card?

Yes, but not with every company or for every car group. Locauto accepts named debit cards, Hertz only for some car groups, and Sicily by Car requires you to buy its protection package. The card must be physical, in the main driver’s name and have enough available balance for the deposit block. Use the “debit card accepted” filter on comparison sites.

Can the rental company add a fee for handling a fine?

The rental company has to pass on the driver’s details, and the fine itself comes from the police or municipality. In 2025 Italy’s consumer authority AGCM challenged automatic “administrative fees” charged to consumers, fining Sicily by Car and Locauto. If a company charges such a fee to your card, ask for the contractual basis and file a complaint if needed.

Where should I avoid parking?

Stay away from yellow-line zones (reserved spaces), ZTL areas without a permit, and pavements. During summer, town centres in Tropea, Pizzo and Scilla are virtually inaccessible by car – use car parks on the outskirts and walk down. A ZTL fine typically starts from about 83 €.
