Calabria can be less expensive for accommodation than Italy’s most popular cities, but prices depend on the season, location and standard of the property. The region offers the full range of places to stay: seaside hotels and intimate B&Bs, agriturismi with home cooking, holiday apartments and campsites right by the beach. Below is a practical guide to accommodation types, the best bases and booking rules.

Types of Accommodation in Calabria

Hotels (2–5 stars) – Classic hotels are found mainly in coastal towns: Tropea, Pizzo, Scalea and the Capo Vaticano area. Three-star properties dominate – they offer air conditioning, breakfast and often a small pool. There are a few luxury 5-star resorts, mostly on the Tyrrhenian coast. Many seaside hotels operate seasonally and close for winter.

B&Bs (Bed & Breakfasts) – This is a popular type of accommodation in Calabria. They are usually family-run, have a few rooms and offer a homemade breakfast, often with local pastries, fresh fruit and espresso. Contact with the hosts can make it easier to choose a restaurant, beach or driving route.

Agriturismo (farm stays) – Farms offering accommodation are a characteristic way to stay in Calabria. Working farms connected with olive, citrus or vine cultivation offer rooms or apartments, along with cooking based on produce from the farm and surrounding area. More on this experience in a dedicated section below.

Holiday apartments and villas – A practical choice for families and longer stays. Available on Booking.com, Airbnb, Vrbo and local rental platforms. Before choosing, check the kitchen equipment, air conditioning, parking and all charges added beyond the base price.

Villaggi turistici and residence – This is an important category on the Calabrian coast, especially in July and August. These bungalow or apartment complexes often have pools, entertainment programmes, children's clubs and sometimes HB/FB or club-style meal plans. They are a common choice for Italian families and an alternative to hotels, B&Bs and private apartments.

Campsites (campeggi) – Calabria has at least several dozen campsites and camping villages, mainly along the coast. They offer tent and campervan pitches, and often mobile homes and bungalows. Examples include Camping Calabrisella (Borgia), Camping Costa Splendente (Le Castella, Isola di Capo Rizzuto), and Village Camping Fico d’India (Le Cannella, Isola di Capo Rizzuto).

Best Areas to Stay

The choice of location depends on your travel plans, access to beaches and whether you are travelling by car.

  • Tropea and the Costa degli Dei – A good base for beaches and nightlife. Sandy cliffs, turquoise water and a wide choice of restaurants attract the largest number of visitors. It is a convenient place for a first trip to Calabria.
  • Reggio Calabria – An urban base with views of Sicily. Museums, the Lungomare promenade and good transport links make it easy to explore the south of the region and take day trips to Sicily – connections operate from Reggio Calabria itself, and also very conveniently from nearby Villa San Giovanni.
  • The Scilla–Pizzo coast – More intimate seaside towns. Scilla, with its fishing quarter of Chianalea, and Pizzo, famous for its tartufo ice cream, offer a quieter atmosphere than Tropea, with equally beautiful beaches.
  • The Sila Mountains – A base for travellers looking for a cooler climate, forests and lakes. Agriturismi and small hotels operate, among other places, near Camigliatello Silano and San Giovanni in Fiore. It is a good destination for hiking and staying away from coastal crowds.
  • Cosenza area – One of the largest cities in the region, with hotels and transport connections. It is a convenient jumping-off point for both the Tyrrhenian and Ionian coasts, as well as the Sila mountains.

When choosing accommodation, decide upfront whether you prefer the Tyrrhenian or Ionian coast. Tyrrhenian towns such as Tropea, Pizzo, Scilla and Praia a Mare are generally better known to tourists, have more beach resorts and better north–south rail connections. The Ionian coast, with places such as Soverato, Isola Capo Rizzuto and Roccella Ionica, is generally quieter but more often requires a car.

Whether you are travelling with or without a car is equally important. Reggio Calabria, Tropea, Pizzo and Scilla are relatively convenient without one thanks to their railway stations, while Capo Vaticano, many inland agriturismi, Sila and parts of Costa degli Aranci and Costa dei Saraceni are much more practical with your own transport.

When and How to Book

The tourist season in Calabria runs from June to September, with the highest prices and lowest availability usually in July and August. The absolute peak is during the first two weeks of August, around Ferragosto. At this time, popular accommodation sells out months before arrival.

Prices depend heavily on the dates, location, standard and booking terms; in July and August they are usually highest in popular resorts.

During peak season, many holiday apartments, houses and residence-type properties operate on weekly turnovers, often Saturday to Saturday or Sunday to Sunday. The minimum stay can then be longer than outside the season. This particularly applies to coastal resort towns and listings on local platforms.

Booking platforms:

  • Booking.com – A wide selection of hotels and B&Bs in the region. Check the free-cancellation terms for the individual listing.
  • Airbnb / Vrbo – Useful for finding apartments and villas, especially for longer stays.
  • Agriturismo.it – A dedicated portal for Italian agriturismi. It allows direct contact and bookings with the property.
  • Campeggi.com / Pitchup.com – Camping portals with listings for pitches, bungalows and camping villages.

When to book: For July and August, book in advance, especially if you are looking for accommodation in Tropea, a popular agriturismo or a campsite. For June and September, the choice is usually wider, but specific dates can still disappear quickly. Outside the season, first check whether the property remains open and which services are operating on site.

Along many parts of the Calabrian coast, some hotels, beach clubs, restaurants and villaggi operate mainly from late spring to early autumn. The full range of services is usually available from June to September, and most fully in July and August. In October and outside the season, accommodation may be available online, but reception, the hotel restaurant or transfers may already operate on a limited basis.

Agriturismo – A Uniquely Calabrian Experience

An agriturismo is not just accommodation, but a form of stay connected with a working farm. Calabrian farm stays combine lodging with an agricultural landscape and cuisine based on produce from the farm itself and the surrounding area.

Under Italian law, an agriturismo is formally linked to a working farm, while accommodation and food service are supplementary to agricultural activity. Regional regulations provide that at least 70% of the value of products and ingredients used for meals and drinks must come from the farm itself and farms in the surrounding area. Up to 25% may come from regional artisanal and agri-food producers, and up to 5% from other sources in cases of objective unavailability. At breakfast, most products may come from outside the farm, but they should be local or regional.

What to expect at an agriturismo:

  • Rooms or apartments in restored farm buildings, often with stone walls and wooden beams
  • Breakfast with local or regional products, depending on the farm’s offering
  • Dinners with menus based on seasonal farm produce; many agriturismi offer them at an additional charge, so check the price, menu scope and drinks directly with the property
  • Olive groves, vineyards, citrus orchards and the opportunity to observe farm work
  • Cooking workshops, if the farm offers them

Agriturismi allow you to stay outside seaside resorts and discover the region’s rural side. Many owners speak mainly Italian, so when booking it is sensible to arrange check-in times, meals and directions. Clusters of agriturismi can be found in the Sila hills, the province of Crotone, the Crati valley near Cosenza and the hills above Tropea.

Tip: Even if you are not staying at an agriturismo, some farms serve dinner to outside guests. Before arriving, confirm availability, price, menu and whether a reservation is required by phone.

Practical Tips

  • Air conditioning – Very useful in summer. Check before booking, as not all budget B&Bs and apartments have it. In mountain agriturismi it is usually unnecessary.
  • Parking – If you are travelling by car, make sure your accommodation has parking. In Tropea and Scilla, especially in the old town, parking can be difficult during peak season.
  • Payments – Smaller B&Bs and agriturismi may prefer cash. Carry some euros, especially inland.
  • Breakfast – It may be included in hotels and B&Bs, but always check the terms of your chosen offer. In apartments, you cook for yourself using local markets and bakeries.
  • Language – Outside larger hotels, English can be limited. Basic Italian or a phone translator helps greatly.
  • Seasonality – Many coastal properties close from November to March. If you are visiting off-season, look for accommodation in Reggio Calabria, Cosenza or Catanzaro – cities that operate year-round.
  • Additional charges – Apartments may have extra charges, for example for final cleaning, bed linen, air conditioning, parking or a security deposit. Before booking, check the full list of charges and the deposit-refund rules.
  • CIR – All accommodation providers in Calabria are required to obtain a CIR (Codice Identificativo Regionale) and display it in all promotional channels, including OTA platforms, Airbnb and social media. Before booking, check whether the CIR number is visible in the listing.
  • ZTL and access to the property – Many historic centres have a ZTL (Zona a Traffico Limitato). In Calabrian old towns and coastal centres, access directly to the property may be restricted by time or reserved for residents, so before arrival check whether your accommodation is in a ZTL, where the nearest public parking is and whether brief access for unloading luggage is possible.
  • Tourist tax (tassa di soggiorno) – Some municipalities charge this fee separately on site. Its amount depends on the municipality, type of property, season and number of nights, so check the rules before booking.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does accommodation cost in Calabria during summer?

Prices depend on the type of accommodation, location, standard, number of guests and booking terms. Peak season is July and August, when prices in popular resorts are usually highest and availability is lowest. Before comparing offers, check whether the price includes breakfast, cleaning, air conditioning, parking and any security deposit.

Is an agriturismo worth it compared to a hotel?

Yes, if you want quieter surroundings and cuisine connected with local agriculture. An agriturismo offers contact with the farm and products that largely come from the property itself and the surrounding area. Meals are not always included with accommodation, so check the breakfast and dinner options before booking.

How far in advance should I book accommodation in Calabria?

For July and August, book in advance, especially around Tropea and at popular agriturismi and campsites. For June and September, the choice can be wider. Remember that during peak season many apartments and residence properties offer stays in weekly blocks.

Which area of Calabria is best as a base?

Tropea and the Costa degli Dei are a good choice for beaches and evening life. Reggio Calabria works as an urban base with convenient access to Sicily – through connections from the city and nearby Villa San Giovanni. Sila is the choice for mountain lovers, hiking and cooler temperatures, while Cosenza makes it easier to travel between the coasts and mountains.

Does Airbnb work in Calabria?

Yes, Airbnb lists apartments and villas in Calabria, especially in coastal towns. Before booking, check the equipment description, additional charges, cancellation rules and whether the CIR number is displayed in the listing.
