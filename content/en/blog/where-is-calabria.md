Calabria is the toe of the Italian boot – the southernmost region of the mainland, a narrow, mountainous peninsula between the Tyrrhenian and Ionian seas, with Sicily just across the water. **By road it begins about 400 km south-east of Rome and 200 km beyond Naples, and it sees far fewer foreign visitors than Puglia or Sicily.** This guide shows where Calabria is, what surrounds it, how it differs from its better-known neighbours and the practical ways of getting there.

Look at a map of Italy and follow the boot down to the tip of the foot: that is Calabria. It has only one land neighbour, the region of Basilicata to the north. Everything else is coast – the Tyrrhenian Sea to the west, the Ionian Sea to the east and south, and at the very tip the Strait of Messina, which separates the mainland from Sicily by little more than 3 km.

The region is about 250 km long but never wide. At the Isthmus of Catanzaro the two seas are only about 30 km apart, and from the hilltop town of Tiriolo you can see both. Between the coasts rise four mountain ranges – Pollino, the Sila, the Serre and the Aspromonte – which is why Calabria has ski lifts as well as beaches.

For a closer look at the provinces, the named coasts and driving distances, see our [map of Calabria](/blog/map-of-calabria/).

## Two seas, two coasts {#two-coasts}

Calabria's two coasts feel like different regions.

**The Tyrrhenian coast (west)** is the one in the photographs: cliffs, coves and towns built on rock above the sea. Its best-known stretch is the Costa degli Dei, the Coast of the Gods, around [Tropea](/destinations/tropea/), [Pizzo](/destinations/pizzo/) and [Capo Vaticano](/nature/capo-vaticano/). Further south, the [Costa Viola](/nature/costa-viola/) leads to [Scilla](/destinations/scilla/) and the Strait of Messina; further north, the Riviera dei Cedri runs past Diamante and Scalea towards Basilicata.

**The Ionian coast (east and south)** is flatter, quieter and far longer: wide beaches, fewer resorts and hill towns set back from the sea – [Gerace](/destinations/gerace/), [Stilo](/destinations/stilo/), [Bova](/destinations/bova/). This was the shore of Magna Graecia, the Greek colonies of southern Italy, and the ruins of Locri Epizefiri and Kaulon stand beside the coast road.

## What Calabria is known for {#what-it-is-known-for}

- **Tropea** – a cliff-top old town above a sandy beach, and the sweet red onion that carries its name (*Cipolla Rossa di Tropea Calabria IGP*).
- **The Riace Bronzes** – two Greek warriors from the 5th century BC, found in the sea in 1972 and displayed at the [National Archaeological Museum in Reggio Calabria](/destinations/reggio-calabria/museo-nazionale/).
- **’Nduja and chilli** – the spreadable, fiery [salami from Spilinga](/cuisine/nduja/) and a cuisine built around *peperoncino*.
- **Bergamot** – more than 90% of the world's [bergamot](/cuisine/bergamot/), the citrus that scents Earl Grey tea, grows on a narrow strip of coast in the province of Reggio Calabria.
- **Three national parks** – [Pollino](/nature/pollino/), the [Sila](/nature/sila/) and the [Aspromonte](/nature/aspromonte/), with mountains rising to 2,267 m within an hour or two of the beach.
- **Greek and Byzantine heritage** – from the temples of Magna Graecia to the tiny 10th-century [Cattolica di Stilo](/destinations/stilo/cattolica/), and villages where a Greek dialect is still spoken.
- **The ’Ndrangheta** – the region's organised crime is real, but it does not target visitors; we look at the figures in [Is Calabria safe?](/blog/is-calabria-safe/)

## Calabria, Puglia or Sicily? {#calabria-or}

The three are often confused, so it helps to place them:

- **Calabria** is the *toe* of the boot.
- **Puglia** (Apulia) is the *heel*, on the opposite side of the Gulf of Taranto. The two regions do not touch – Basilicata lies between them.
- **Sicily** is the island the toe appears to be kicking, across the Strait of Messina.

In character, Calabria is the most mountainous and the least developed for tourism of the three. Puglia is flatter, easier to drive and far better known abroad; Sicily is larger, with bigger cities and more headline sights. Calabria's draw is the combination of sea and mountains at short range, lower prices outside August and the fact that, away from Tropea, most of the other visitors are Italian.

## How to get to Calabria {#getting-there}

**By air.** The main airport is Lamezia Terme (SUF), in the middle of the region and about an hour's drive from Tropea. Reggio Calabria (REG) serves the south; Crotone (CRV) has a handful of mostly domestic routes. As of October 2026, Ryanair's route list includes London Stansted to both Lamezia Terme and Reggio Calabria – a flight of roughly three hours – but many routes are seasonal, so check the dates. From elsewhere, the usual way is a domestic connection through Rome or Milan.

**By train.** High-speed Frecciarossa and Italo trains run down the Tyrrhenian coast from Rome and Naples. Rome to Lamezia Terme takes about 4 to 4½ hours and Rome to Reggio Calabria about 5½ to 6 hours; slower Intercity trains add around two hours. For Tropea, change at Lamezia Terme or Rosarno to a regional train.

**By car.** From Naples, the A2 reaches northern Calabria in under three hours. A car is close to essential once you arrive if you want to see hill towns such as Gerace, Stilo or Bova – see [car rental in Calabria](/practical/car-rental/).

**From Sicily.** Ferries cross from Messina to Villa San Giovanni around the clock; foot passengers can also take the fast boat to Reggio Calabria.

Full details, including transfers from each airport, are in our guide to [getting to Calabria](/practical/getting-there/).

## Where to start {#where-to-start}

If this is your first visit, three areas make sense as a first base:

- **Tropea and the Coast of the Gods** – the classic choice for beaches, an hour from Lamezia airport.
- **Reggio Calabria and Scilla** – for the Riace Bronzes, the seafront facing Sicily and the fishing quarter of Chianalea.
- **The Ionian hill towns** – Gerace and Stilo, for Byzantine churches and quiet beaches.

Our list of [25 things to do in Calabria](/blog/things-to-do-in-calabria/) covers all three, and [Calabria weather by month](/blog/calabria-weather-by-month/) will help you pick the season. If you have a week, the [7-day itinerary](/practical/itinerary-7-days/) links them into one route.
